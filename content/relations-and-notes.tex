\subsection{Relationship map}
\begin{itemize}

  \item \Character{usurper} is the main magic caster from the family.
  
  \item \Character{usurper} has tricked \Character{vampirehead} in adding a tatoo everytime a new vampire is created. It is true that the tatoo gives vampires the capability of sending mental messages ignoring space, but it also lets the \Character{usurper} spy in them when this is happening.
  
  \item \Character{usurper} wants to become the new head. For this, it wants to create trouble for the other vampires, specially \Character{vampirehead}. While doing it, it wants to appear as good friends with everyone too.
  
  \item If \Character{vampirehead} leaves the position, the next in line is \Character{usurper}. This is revealed in \Clue{vampireranking}.

  \item  \Character{enforcer} is responsible for enforcing the rules, having a similar authority to the head. They are the only two supposed to have a full understanding of the family hierarchy. \Character{usurper} also knows.
  
  \item \Character{ghoul} is treated as a pet animal, a jester. It wants revenge.
  
  \item \Character{ghoul} is a direct subordinate of \Character{enforcer}, and has to do its bidding. Only they are supposed to knows this, as it gives the capability of \Character{enforcer} a chance to use it as a spy. \Character{usurper} knows this.
  
  \item \Character{ghoul} steals a ritual from \Character{usurper}. (\Character{usurper} in truth knows of this, and just pretends to not know).
  
  \item \Character{ghoul} uses the ritual to look into the future, noting that \Character{newcomer} dies. Forcing to follow the rules, it discloses this information to \Character{enforcer}.

  \item A vampire having a relationship with a human is against rules. This is specified in \Clue{rulebook}.
  
  \item A vampire that breaks the rules is either exiled or killed.
  
  \item The \Clue{rulebook} \Character{enforcer} uses is stored away behind \Clue{rocks}, using a password.
  
  \item \Character{lover} was having a secret relation with \Character{newcomer}.

  \item \Character{prevlover} had a secret relation with \Character{newcomer}, but \Character{newcomer} left \Character{prevlover} to be with \Character{lover}.

  \item \Character{newcomer} was using \Character{vampirehead} to gain immortality, powers, and to be able to be with \Character{lover}.
      
  \item \Character{traitor} is in cahoots with the \outsideentity{church} and hates vampires.
  
  \item \Character{traitor} is trying to get a flask of the \Clue{forbiddenpotion} to give to all the vampires, hence killing them.

  \item \Character{traitor} pleaded \Character{newcomer} to abandon the idea of becoming a vampire.
  
  \item \Character{supremacist} thinks that every human is inferior, and is trying to make every human into mindless cattle.
  
  \item \Character{traitor} and \Character{supremacist} have frequent fights regarding the value of vampires in the world throughout the years.
  
  \item \Character{ghoul} is treated as a pet animal, a jester. It wants revenge.
  
  \item \Character{buttler} is \Character{vampirehead}'s buttler.

  \item \Character{vampirehead} wants to turn \Character{newcomer} into a vampire due to being in love with him, so she can confess and have a relationship without breaking rules.
  
  \item \Character{usurper} turns \Character{newcomer} into a vampire without its knowledge, using the \Clue{forbiddenpotion}.

  \item \Character{vampirehead} gives \Character{newcomer} the \Clue{forbiddenpotion}, leading him to die minutes later.
  
  \item The main mystery is \Mystery{who-killed-newcomer}.

\end{itemize}
