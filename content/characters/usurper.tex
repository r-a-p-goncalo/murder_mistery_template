\paragraph{Background}

\timestamp{Three thousand years ago}, I was a human hungry for magic and power. I devised the blood potion that lets a willing human join a vampire family, and \Character{vampirehead} made me its first companion. I also learned the potion's most useful secret: given to a vampire, it kills. I have spent centuries studying the arts that can turn that knowledge into power.

I know \Character{vampirehead} grows suspicious whenever my research makes a mess, so I make myself indispensable and pleasant. I slipped the succession ranking into the family's records, ensuring that its powers would pass to me if the head fell. I also convinced it to give new vampires communication tattoos. The tattoos work exactly as promised, but I can listen whenever the marked vampires use them.

I offered to help prepare this gathering and made sure the others knew my study would be empty. Let them steal from it and make themselves look guilty. \timestamp{Yesterday, at 12:00}, I paid \Character{buttler} to replace \Character{supremacist}'s poisoned wine with my own. \Character{newcomer} drank the potion and became a vampire without realising it. When \Character{vampirehead} performs the turning ritual \timestamp{today}, its potion will kill a vampire. The head will bear the blame, and I must make sure no one proves my part in it.

\paragraph{GM Notes}

\Character{usurper} is the actual killer. Its plan is to secretly turn \Character{newcomer} with the lunch wine, then let \Character{vampirehead} administer a correct second potion and appear responsible for the death. The strongest route to the solution combines \Character{buttler}'s account of the bottle swap, the double-dose effect in the forbidden-potion instructions, and \Clue{vampireranking}, which gives \Character{usurper} a succession motive. It should deny everything, redirect suspicion to the poison, the church plot, and the head, and never volunteer its knowledge of the first dose.
