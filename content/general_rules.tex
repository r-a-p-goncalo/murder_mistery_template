
\section{General rules}

\subsection{Game Master}
The Game Master (GM) guides the game and has the last word. Even when the GM also plays a character, they may clarify or adjust a rule when necessar. The GM resolves uncertainty, tracks points where applicable, and fills gaps that the written material does not cover.

\subsection{Character}
Each player acts as a character during the game. A character sheet gives that player information and incentives, rather than a script that must be followed word for word.

Note that meta actions are prohibited except when required by a game component. For example, if you wanted to reveal a secret of yours, you would act on it, not show your character sheet.

\ObjectivesHeading{Objectives}
Objectives are outcomes a character is rewarded for achieving. Some may be revealed only during the game. The reward for completing an objective is usually shown by \PositiveReward{1} and, if there is a negative reward for a player failing, it will be shown with \NegativeReward{1}.

\LosingConditionsHeading{Losing conditions}
Losing conditions are outcomes that disqualify a character from the final score, even if that character completed objectives worth points.

\SecretsHeading{Secrets}
Secrets are facts that other players may discover.

If a secret has the reward in the form of (\NegativeReward{1}, \PositiveReward{1}), it is showing first the negative reward this character receives if at least one other plays finds out this secret (\NegativeReward{1}) and then showing the positive reward awarded for players that find this secret (\PositiveReward{1}).

If a secret has the form of (\PositiveReward{1}, \NegativeReward{1}), it is fake. For each other player that says this is this character's secret, this character receives \PositiveReward{1}. Each of those players then receive the penalty \NegativeReward{1}. 

This is only processed at the end of the game.

\ActionsHeading{Actions}
Actions are behaviors or tasks the player should perform, or at least strongly consider, during the game. They give the roleplay texture and help move the story forward.

\BackgroundHeading{Background}
The background provides the character's history before the event. It may include narrative context, leads for the main mystery, information other players are seeking, and facts the character wants to conceal.

\subsection{How to play}
The group is trying to solve the main mystery, which should become clear enough to answer before the session ends.

This usually ends up in a final vote, where players will have to vote out a villain.

Stay in character as much as possible, avoiding meta-actions. Treat the background as real, follow your actions, and take your secrets seriously. Do not read your sheet aloud or reveal highly specific game information except when asking the GM for clarification. Use a private message or quiet conversation when the GM needs to resolve something secretly.

Points create incentives to complete secondary objectives, control what information you share, and investigate other characters. Do not lose sight of the losing conditions. It does not make sense to protect your secrets to the point of losing the game. When a secret is exposed, the affected character has less reason to hide that fact and may become more proactive. 

Don't forget that lying is an option. Players can reveal useful information while protecting themselves with a plausible alternative explanation. 

Clues may also be hidden in the physical spaces, so players should be encouraged to search and interact with the setting.

At the end of the game, after the vote, each player will have the opportunity to, privately, tell the GM \textbf{5} secrets they think are true. Only after this are the points counted.
