\subsection{Complete background}

This is the GM-only account of what actually happened. It distinguishes the true chain of events from the suspicions and partial information available to the players.

\paragraph{Long before the party}

\Character{vampirehead} was once a simple human, \timestamp{thousands of years ago}. One day, while looking at the moon and stars and dreaming of freedom, that changed. Freedom from hunger; freedom to cruise the skies and go anywhere; freedom from the pain of dealing with other humans. \Character{vampirehead} desired detachment from humanity, and something heard. From that moment on, \Character{vampirehead} was a vampire: free from all, but hated by all. The world hated them so fiercely that the sun itself threatened their existence.

\Character{vampirehead} grew lonely, then lonelier, then lonelier still, until they decided to start a family.

One day, \timestamp{3 thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} met \Character{usurper}, a human with a keen interest in magic and a strong desire for power. \Character{usurper} devised a potion made with a vampire's blood that, when given to a willing human, turns that human into family. \Character{vampirehead} could add further terms to the contract, but that did not seem to interest them.

\Character{usurper} knows that, if this potion is given to a vampire, it will kill it.

Everything seemed well for \timestamp{a thousand years}, with all the things two vampires could do for fun. But \Character{vampirehead} was displeased by \Character{usurper}'s thirst for power. \Character{usurper} seemed like a good family member, but it was always obvious that its priority lay in the mystic arts.

This became clear as research took over \Character{usurper}'s life. There was always a mess in the house, artifacts scattered about, and things disappearing. \Character{usurper} was pleased that the chaos made it difficult for \Character{vampirehead} to spy on the research being done, as disorder did not please the head.

\timestamp{2 thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} introduced \Character{enforcer} to the family to enforce its rules. Its word would be second only to \Character{vampirehead}'s in upholding the rules that \Character{vampirehead} devised then and there, writing them into a book: \Clue{rulebook}.

This book gained magical properties: if a family vote finds that a rule has been broken, the vampire that broke it is automatically stripped of its powers. This showed \Character{usurper} how powerful an original vampire could be, capable of creating magical artifacts almost by accident.

Later, \Character{usurper} learned that, if \Character{vampirehead} were stripped of its powers and the family had a succession ranking, those powers would pass to the next in line. To that end, \Character{usurper} buried \Clue{vampireranking} among a million other documents, and \Character{vampirehead} accepted it without much attention. Now, if the head falls, \Character{usurper} will receive its powers. The scheming had begun.


\Character{usurper} devised several magical tattoos. Two were relevant: one allowed its wearer to listen to the tattooed, and the other allowed the tattooed to communicate with the bearer of the parent tattoo. \Character{usurper} presented only the second tattoo to \Character{vampirehead}. Its stated purpose was genuine, but it also allowed \Character{usurper} to eavesdrop whenever those messages were used.

\timestamp{Decades after the death of Christ}, the family needed better household and financial management, so \Character{buttler}, a merchant known for both competence and greed, was invited.

\timestamp{In 100 AD}, \Character{enforcer} met a charming jester while travelling. It was an artistic, charismatic soul with which \Character{enforcer} fell in love. As vampires could not have relationships with humans under the rules, \Character{enforcer} asked \Character{vampirehead} to turn the jester, \Character{ghoul}, into a vampire.

But \Character{enforcer} wanted more: someone who would certainly follow its rules. It therefore asked \Character{vampirehead} to make the potion with \Character{enforcer}'s blood instead. In this way, \Character{enforcer} could add a term to the contract: ''\Character{ghoul} will always follow \Character{enforcer}'s instructions when given as orders.''

At the turning ceremony, \Character{buttler} noticed that the flask given to \Character{ghoul} did not contain the potion. When this was brought up privately with \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{vampirehead} paid for \Character{buttler}'s silence.

As the years went by, several new family members joined.

\Character{lover} was a strong, imposing warrior who would serve as the family's protector. It joined \timestamp{in 200 AD}.

\Character{supremacist} was a terrible dictator who treated the vampires well. It joined \timestamp{in 400 AD}.

\Character{prevlover} was an interesting investigator and a useful household member whenever mysteries arose. It joined \timestamp{in 1000 AD}.

\Character{lover} and \Character{traitor} were in love, so \Character{traitor} joined the family, though it was somewhat controversial. After all, \Character{traitor} was a good Samaritan who went around doing good deeds. The thought was that immortality would allow for even more good deeds, though that was obviously a stretch. \Character{traitor} joined \timestamp{in 1400 AD}.

At some point, \Character{traitor} took issue with the nature of its new family and began conspiring with the \outsideentity{church}. For \Character{traitor}, vampires should be cured or, better still, destroyed, while humans---especially poor people from other lands---should be taken in.

This stands in sharp contrast to \Character{supremacist}, who increasingly believes that all humans should be killed or turned into cattle. \Character{supremacist} especially hates poor humans without authority entering countries that are not theirs. The good times, it believes, were when kings ruled.

Throughout the years, \Character{usurper} offers help to any vampire who needs it and comes to be known as the cute helper.

\Character{ghoul} is seen as a joke rather than the jokester, and grows resentful.

\paragraph{During the last two years}

\timestamp{Two years ago}, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{vampirehead} met. \Character{vampirehead} was growing lonelier, so it invited \Character{newcomer} as a guest to a party, where \Character{newcomer} met the other vampires for the first time.

Everyone knew that \Character{vampirehead} was courting \Character{newcomer}, and that it was only a matter of time before \Character{newcomer} joined the family.

But behind the scenes, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{prevlover} began a romantic relationship.

\timestamp{A year ago}, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{lover} also began a relationship. \Character{newcomer} then left \Character{prevlover}, saying, ''What we're doing is wrong.''

As these affairs unfold, \Character{vampirehead} remains oblivious, making an effort to keep its distance from \Character{newcomer} so as not to break the rules.

\timestamp{A couple of weeks before the party}, \Character{usurper} asked whether \Character{vampirehead} would like help preparing it. \Character{vampirehead} appreciated the offer and asked whether any vampire besides \Character{usurper}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{newcomer} would like to come the day before to help. Everyone refused, and some noticed that \Character{usurper} would not be in the study, making it a good opportunity to steal artifacts.

\paragraph{The day before the party}

\timestamp{At 01:00}, several vampires enter \Character{usurper}'s study.

\Character{ghoul} goes looking for a way to attain freedom from \Character{enforcer}. \Character{ghoul} finds a ritual titled ''To change the future'' and steals it.

\Character{prevlover} goes looking for a way to be with \Character{newcomer} again. Some artifacts only work on humans. It finds a ritual titled ''To change and know a person'' and steals it. \Character{ghoul} notices this.

\Character{traitor} goes looking for the forbidden potion, but fails to find it.

\Character{supremacist} steals a poison that only works on humans.

\timestamp{At 8:00}, \Character{supremacist} poisons the wine. \Character{buttler} notices \Character{supremacist} as it leaves. \Character{supremacist} tells \Character{buttler} that it was not in the mood to share a space with a mere human and pays \Character{buttler} for its silence.

\Character{vampirehead}, \Character{newcomer}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{usurper} have lunch together at \timestamp{12:00}. \Character{usurper} had paid \Character{buttler} to replace the poisoned wine with wine of its own---wine that was supposedly ``better.'' That wine contained the forbidden potion.

\timestamp{At 15:00}, \Character{ghoul} performs the ritual and looks into the future. It sees that \Character{newcomer} will die, though that alone deserves little concern. Nevertheless, because of a previous order from \Character{enforcer}, \Character{ghoul} must disclose all information it knows about the family. \Character{ghoul} does not immediately realise it, but this shows that \Character{newcomer} has already been turned.

\Character{enforcer} wants to prevent any problems, but does not want to tell \Character{vampirehead} that people have been stealing from \Character{usurper}. \Character{enforcer} calls \Character{prevlover} and says it will overlook the theft if \Character{prevlover} hands over the ritual. \Character{enforcer} wants to use it on \Character{newcomer} to find clues as to why \Character{newcomer} would be killed.

\Character{prevlover} panics and wipes \Character{enforcer}'s memory. \Character{enforcer} is left with a sense that something is wrong, but no longer remembers the warning, the ritual, or the agreement.
