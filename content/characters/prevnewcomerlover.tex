\paragraph{Background}

\timestamp{In 1000 AD}, I joined the family because I was an investigator with a gift for untangling mysteries. \timestamp{Two years ago}, I became close to \Character{newcomer}. Then, \timestamp{a year ago}, it left me, saying only that what we were doing was wrong. I have never accepted that as a real explanation.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 01:00}, I entered \Character{usurper}'s study looking for a way to understand \Character{newcomer} and win it back. I stole a ritual called ``To change and know a person,'' which can peer into a human's memories and alter them. \Character{ghoul} saw me take it.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 15:00}, \Character{enforcer} confronted me. It offered to overlook my theft if I handed over the ritual so it could inspect \Character{newcomer}'s memories. I panicked. I used the ritual to wipe \Character{enforcer}'s memory instead. I did it to protect myself, but now I cannot stop wondering what it had learned and whether \Character{newcomer}'s death is connected to it.

\paragraph{GM Notes}

\Character{prevlover} is a strong suspect because it had a personal motive, stole memory magic, and erased \Character{enforcer}'s memory. It did not kill \Character{newcomer}; it acted only to conceal its theft and its continuing attachment. The GM should let evidence of the erased memory expose this wrongdoing while making clear that it occurred after \Character{usurper}'s wine was served.
