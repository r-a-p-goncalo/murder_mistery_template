\BackgroundHeading{Background}

\timestamp{In 500 AD}, I ruled humans who mistook obedience for dignity and kindness for strength. I learned that a stable realm needs a clear hierarchy, firm hands, and people who know the limits of their station. Then I met \Character{vampirehead}. Her power was absolute, her family answered to her will, and she made no apology for standing above humanity. I fell in love with her before I had the courage to name it.

I treated the vampires in her circle with every courtesy due to their rank. \Character{vampirehead} noticed. She brought me into the family and gave me a responsibility worthy of me: I would decide whether a human was suitable to join it. I have never believed that immortality should be handed to anyone who asks. A new vampire should strengthen the family, not dilute it.

The party I joined and drank that potion was the best of night of my life. \Character{usurper}, \Character{enforcer}, \Character{buttler} and \Character{ghoul} were already in the family.

\timestamp{In 700 AD}, \Character{lover} joined as a protector. I accepted his place because he offered a clear service.

\timestamp{In 1000 AD}, \Character{prevlover} became the family's investigator. I accepted his place because he offered a clear service.

\timestamp{In 1400 AD}, \Character{traitor} arrived. \Character{lover} pleaded for her, and \Character{vampirehead} accepted despite my objections. Since then, \Character{traitor} has preached about human suffering as if our family were meant to kneel before every frightened mortal. We have argued for centuries.

\timestamp{Two years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} introduced \Character{newcomer} at a family gathering. I saw at once what she wanted. She looked at him with an affection that made my old restraint feel foolish. He may be charming, but charm is not worthiness. When the head asked for my judgment, I said he was unfit to join us. She was so determined to admit him that she threatened to remove my authority if I continued to stand in the way.

\timestamp{Two months ago}, \Character{vampirehead} announced that \Character{newcomer} would join the family at a party on \timestamp{16/7/2026}. Every vampire was expected to attend. I did not approve, but I obeyed the summons.

\timestamp{A couple of weeks before the party}, \Character{usurper} publicly offered to help prepare the celebration. \Character{vampirehead} then asked whether anyone besides \Character{usurper}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{newcomer} wished to arrive two nights early and help as well. I declined. The invitation made it clear that \Character{usurper}'s study would be unattended on the morning before the party.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 01:00}, I entered that study. It was the usual chaos of notes, bottles, and half-finished magical experiments. I took a vial that I believed contained a poison harmless to vampires but fatal to humans. I think there was more to it, but I can't quite remember.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 08:00}, I mixed half the vial into a bottle of wine intended for \Character{newcomer}. I don't really remember specifics about the wine. I kept the remaining half of the poison. Nothing useful should be wasted. When I encountered \Character{buttler} while leaving, I told her I had reconsidered \Character{newcomer} and wanted him to receive a better bottle as a welcome. I said that I wanted my change of heart to stay a secret, and so I paid her for her silence.

\timestamp{At 17:00}, the vial I had disappeared without me noticing. Did someone steal it from me?

\GMNotes{

\Character{supremacist} made a deliberate attempt on \Character{newcomer}'s life, making her an intentional and credible red herring. She believed the stolen potion would kill a human, but it was actually a truth potion and did not kill him. \Character{buttler} was about to serve the wine set aside for \Character{newcomer} at lunch when \Character{usurper} publicly replaced it with her own bottle. The poisoned wine therefore never reached \Character{newcomer}.

\paragraph{Why would someone suspect this character?}

\Character{supremacist} openly opposed \Character{newcomer}'s admission, had the opportunity to enter \Character{usurper}'s study, poisoned a bottle intended for him, and paid \Character{buttler} for silence. Her jealousy of \Character{vampirehead}'s interest in him gives the attempt an additional personal motive.

\paragraph{How can innocence of this character be proved?}

\Character{buttler} can establish the crucial lunch sequence: she was preparing to serve the bottle reserved for \Character{newcomer}, then \Character{usurper} substituted a supposedly finer wine. The half-empty vial and the poisoned wine show that \Character{supremacist} tried to kill a human, but the later evidence that \Character{newcomer} had already become a vampire explains why neither her potion nor her bottle caused his death.

\paragraph{What is this character's role in the story and game?}

\Character{supremacist} supplies a morally compromised but innocent suspect. Her attempt creates a believable alternative explanation for the death, while her memories of the public invitation explain why several vampires could access \Character{usurper}'s study. Her jealousy and rigid political beliefs also give the player strong material for confrontation with \Character{vampirehead} and \Character{traitor}.

\paragraph{How can this character point into the intended solution?}

Once pressed about the wine, \Character{supremacist} can reveal that a bottle had been prepared for \Character{newcomer} before lunch. \Character{buttler}'s testimony then identifies \Character{usurper} as the person who changed that plan. This separates the failed poisoning from the real first dose and directs the investigation toward \Character{usurper}'s wine.
