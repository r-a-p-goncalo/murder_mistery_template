\CharacterSheet{enforcer}{Enforcer}{

\shortdescription{The guardian of the family's laws, entrusted with authority second only to the head.}

\paragraph{Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Find who killed the newcomer.
  
  \item Locate the \Clue{rulebook}, and list the rules that were broken by the elements of the family.
  
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Secrets}
\begin{itemize}
  \item I've lost my memory and don't remember where the rulebook is.

\end{itemize}


\paragraph{Actions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Be cold and make sure that everyone is being well-behaved and rule-abiding.
  
  \item If \Character{ghoul} is not doing what you want, make it an order. That way, obedience is the only option.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Background}

I joined this family \timestamp{two thousand years ago} because \Character{vampirehead} needed someone to enforce its laws. I helped make those laws real. The \Clue{rulebook} carries the family's judgement: when the family proves that one of us has broken a rule, the offender loses their powers. I have always taken that responsibility seriously.

\timestamp{In 100 AD}, I fell in love with a travelling jester: \Character{ghoul}. Because a vampire may not love a human, I asked \Character{vampirehead} to turn it. I also insisted that the turning potion use my blood, so I could bind \Character{ghoul} to obey any instruction I give as an order. That bond is secret, useful, and mine to command.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 15:00}, \Character{ghoul} came to me with a warning about \Character{newcomer}'s death. I remember deciding not to involve \Character{vampirehead} until I understood why people had been stealing from \Character{usurper}'s study. I went to speak with \Character{prevlover}, who had taken a ritual that could reveal a human's memories. After that conversation, my memory becomes a blank. I know something important was taken from me, and I need to recover it before the family pays the price.

\paragraph{GM Notes}

\Character{enforcer} is innocent but its missing memory protects \Character{prevlover} and delays the investigation. Its authority, the missing \Clue{rulebook}, and its ability to order \Character{ghoul} make it a plausible suspect. If players uncover the memory ritual or confront \Character{prevlover}, confirm that \Character{enforcer} was trying to prevent a death, not cause one.


}
