\subsubsection{Complete background}

\paragraph{Long before the party}

\Character{vampirehead} was once a simple human, \timestamp{thousands of years ago}. One day, while looking at the moon and stars and dreaming of freedom, that changed. Freedom from hunger; freedom to cruise the skies and go anywhere; freedom from the pain of dealing with other humans. \Character{vampirehead} desired detachment from humanity, and something heard. From that moment on, \Character{vampirehead} was a vampire: free from all, but hated by all. The world hated them so fiercely that the sun itself threatened their existence.

\Character{vampirehead} grew lonely, then lonelier, then lonelier still, until they decided to start a family.

One day, \timestamp{3 thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} met \Character{usurper}, a human with a keen interest in magic and a strong desire for power. \Character{usurper} devised a potion made with a vampire's blood that, when given to a willing human, turns that human into family. \Character{vampirehead} could add further terms to the contract, but that did not seem to interest them.

Everything seemed well for \timestamp{a thousand years}, with all the things two vampires could do for fun. But \Character{vampirehead} was displeased by \Character{usurper}'s thirst for power. \Character{usurper} seemed like a good family member, but it was always obvious that its priority lay in the mystic arts.

This became clear as research took over \Character{usurper}'s life. There was always a mess in the house, artifacts scattered about, and things disappearing. \Character{usurper} was pleased that the chaos made it difficult for \Character{vampirehead} to spy on the research being done, as disorder did not please the head. \Character{usurper} even encrypted most of the information on its notes.

\timestamp{2 thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} introduced \Character{enforcer} to the family to enforce its rules. Its word would be second only to \Character{vampirehead}'s in upholding the rules that \Character{vampirehead} devised then and there, writing them into a book: \Clue{rulebook}.

This book gained magical properties: if a family vote finds that a rule has been broken, the vampire that broke it is automatically stripped of its powers. This showed \Character{usurper} how powerful an original vampire could be, capable of creating magical artifacts almost by accident.

By accident, due to the written rules, \Character{vampirehead} made it so that, if it lost its powers, \Character{usurper} would inherit them. \Character{usurper} noticed this.

These rules would be all easily remembered by \Character{enforcer}, and so it hid the \Clue{rulebook} in the castle. They were public rules, and \Character{enforcer} would in the future repeat some of them at gatherings.

% At the present day, no vampire actually remembers the rules well due to the ritual

\timestamp{Decades after the death of Christ}, the family needed better household and financial management, so \Character{buttler}, a merchant known for both competence and greed, was invited. It did its job well. \Character{enforcer} was always commenting how it wanted its own buttler.

\timestamp{In 100 AD}, \Character{enforcer} met a charming jester while travelling. It was an artistic, charismatic soul with which \Character{enforcer} fell in love. As vampires could not have relationships with humans under the rules, \Character{enforcer} asked \Character{vampirehead} to turn the jester, \Character{ghoul}, into a vampire.

But \Character{enforcer} wanted more: someone who would certainly follow its rules. It therefore asked \Character{vampirehead} to make the potion with \Character{enforcer}'s blood instead. In this way, \Character{enforcer} could add a term to the contract: ''\Character{ghoul} will always follow \Character{enforcer}'s instructions when given as orders.''. The turning was done in secret, with only \Character{enforcer} and \Character{vampirehead} present. \Character{vampirehead} did not want the other vampires to get the same idea.

Because of \Character{enforcer}'s position, it has the need to know about all the other vampires, and so, it gives the order to \Character{ghoul}: "I order you to tell me any relevant information you find about our family's elements, whenever you find it, for all of time".

At the turning ceremony, \Character{buttler} noticed that the flask given to \Character{ghoul} did not contain the potion. When this was brought up privately with \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{vampirehead} paid for \Character{buttler}'s silence.

As the years went by, several new family members joined.

Most of them made schemes, betrayed \Character{vampirehead}'s values, and ended up having to be exiled or killed. This showed \Character{usurper} that dethroning \Character{vampirehead} was no simple matter.

\Character{usurper} was responsible for executing the specially gruesome traitors when \Character{enforcer} was not available kill them. \Character{usurper} found out when experimenting in this process, that, if the forbidden potion is given to a vampire, it will kill it.

\Character{supremacist} was a terrible dictator who treated the vampires well. The reason was that it was falling in love with \Character{vampirehead}. It joined \timestamp{in 500 AD}. \Character{vampirehead} was afraid of making non-suited humans join the family and so, as \Character{supremacist} was so hateful of them, gave it the responsibility to be the final judge of if a human could or not join the family.

\Character{lover} was a strong, imposing warrior who would serve as the family's protector. It joined \timestamp{in 700 AD}.

\Character{prevlover} was an interesting investigator. It joined \timestamp{in 1000 AD}.

\Character{lover} and \Character{traitor} were in love, so \Character{traitor} joined the family, though it was somewhat controversial. After all, \Character{traitor} was a good Samaritan who went around doing good deeds. The thought was that immortality would allow for even more good deeds, though that was obviously a stretch. \Character{traitor} joined \timestamp{in 1400 AD}.

At some point, \Character{traitor} took issue with the nature of its new family and began conspiring with the \outsideentity{church}. For \Character{traitor}, vampires should be cured or, better still, destroyed, while humans, especially poor people from other lands, should be taken in.

Everyone knows of \Character{traitor}'s wild opinions, and thinks of it as a hypocrite. \Character{usurper} actually tells, as a "joke", that if a vampire drinks the forbidden potion a second time, it will die, and that \Character{traitor} should do it. This is a drunken accident, \Character{usurper} is actually afraid that \Character{traitor} kills \Character{vampirehead} before its powers can be inherited.

% traitor may become a target of suspition

This stands in sharp contrast to \Character{supremacist}, who increasingly believes that all humans should be killed or turned into cattle. \Character{supremacist} especially hates poor humans without authority entering countries that are not theirs. The good times, it believes, were when kings ruled.

Throughout the years, \Character{usurper} offers help to any vampire who needs it and comes to be known as the cute helper.

\Character{ghoul} is seen as a joke rather than the jokester, and grows resentful. It wants freedom, and stops loving \Character{enforcer}. Actually, it yearns for its death, as that would bring an end to the obligation of following orders.

% Ghoul may become a target of suspition

\Character{supremacist} never had the courage to confess to \Character{vampirehead}.

\paragraph{During the last two years}

\timestamp{Two years ago}, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{vampirehead} met. \Character{vampirehead} was growing lonelier, so it invited \Character{newcomer} as a guest to a party, where \Character{newcomer} met the other vampires for the first time.

Everyone suspected that \Character{vampirehead} was courting \Character{newcomer}, and that it was only a matter of time before \Character{newcomer} joined the family. The only reason that they probably were not already together was because it was against the rules.

\Character{supremacist} did not see why \Character{newcomer} should be added. It was envious of the attention. \Character{vampirehead} was very adamant of making \Character{newcomer} an element of the family, even threatening \Character{supremacist} of stripping its powers.

% supremacist may become a target of suspition

But behind the scenes, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{prevlover} began a romantic relationship.

\timestamp{A year ago}, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{lover} also began a relationship. \Character{newcomer} then left \Character{prevlover}, saying, ''What we're doing is wrong.''

As these affairs unfold, \Character{vampirehead} remains oblivious, making an effort to keep its distance from \Character{newcomer} so as not to break the rules.

\Character{traitor} is suspicious of \Character{lover}, and is afraid that it knows of its plans. \Character{lover} still loves \Character{traitor}.

\timestamp{A couple of weeks before the party}, \Character{usurper} asked whether \Character{vampirehead} would like help preparing it. \Character{vampirehead} appreciated the offer and asked whether any vampire besides \Character{usurper}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{newcomer} would like to come the day before to help. Everyone refused, and some noticed that \Character{usurper} would not be in the study, making it a good opportunity to steal artifacts.

\paragraph{The day before the party}

\timestamp{At 01:00}, several vampires enter \Character{usurper}'s study.

\Character{ghoul} goes looking for a way to attain freedom from \Character{enforcer}. \Character{ghoul} finds a ritual titled ''To change the future'' (this will later produce \Clue{clue:futritualleftovers}) and steals it. 

\Character{prevlover} goes looking for a way to be with \Character{newcomer} again. It finds a ritual titled ''To change and know a person'' (this will later produce \Clue{clue:memritualleftovers}) and steals it. \Character{ghoul} and \Character{prevlover} see eachother. \Character{prevlover} lets go of its artifact that helps it solving mysteries, \Clue{clue:truthseeing}.

\Character{traitor} goes looking for the forbidden potion, but fails to find it. Because of that, it finds an alternative, a ritual titled ''Make what is theirs someone else's'' (this will later produce \Clue{clue:stealritualleftovers}). It assumes it is a ritual to steal something, and may use it to later steal the forbidden potion and execute its plan. In truth, this ritual will displace all the items of the target.

\Character{supremacist} steals a poison that only works on humans, \Clue{clue:truth_potion}, meaning that it kills humans but not vampires. It is actually a truth potion for vampires, one that gives the vampire that asks questions after drinking the potion the power to command the truth from other sentient beings.


\timestamp{At 8:00}, \Character{supremacist} poisons the wine, producing \Clue{clue:poisoned_wine}. When leaving, it finds \Character{buttler} and goes on a long rant about how \Character{newcomer} is not actually that bad and maybe it will be a nice addition to the family. It actually wants it to drink a better wine that the one \Character{vampirehead} usually gives, and so it left a special wine that should only be drank by \Character{newcomer}. It tells \Character{buttler} that it does not want people to know that it has been here or in its change of heart, and so it pays it for its silence regarding it being here and the wine. As only half of the poison was needed and \Character{supremacist} may want to use it later, it only used half. This produces \Clue{clue:truth_potion}.


\Character{vampirehead}, \Character{newcomer}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{usurper} have lunch together at \timestamp{12:00}. When \Character{buttler} was about to serve the wine, \Character{usurper} stopped it and said it had brought its own quality wine. This had the forbidden potion. 

\timestamp{At 15:00}, \Character{ghoul} performs the ritual and looks into the future. It sees that \Character{newcomer} will die, though that alone deserves little concern. This produces \Clue{clue:futritualleftovers}. Because of the order from \Character{enforcer}, \Character{ghoul} must disclose all information it knows about the family. \Character{ghoul} does not immediately realize it, but this shows that \Character{newcomer} has already been turned.

\Character{enforcer} wants to prevent any problems, but does not want to tell \Character{vampirehead} that people have been stealing from \Character{usurper}, as that includes \Character{ghoul}.

\timestamp{At 16:00}, \Character{enforcer} calls \Character{prevlover} and says it will overlook the theft if \Character{prevlover} hands over the ritual. \Character{enforcer} wants to use it on \Character{newcomer} to find clues as to why \Character{newcomer} would be killed. This would mean \Character{enforcer} finding how that \Character{prevlover} and \Character{lover} had a relationship, which would mean \Character{prevlover}'s loss of powers.

\timestamp{At 16:15}, \Character{prevlover} panics and executes the ritual to \Character{enforcer}'s memory. This produces \Clue{clue:memritualleftovers}.

This has the unintended result of wiping random memories from all the vampires. Most memories are regarding \Character{usurper}'s study and other artifacts, such as the rules of the \Clue{rulebook} that were forgotten.
\begin{itemize}

    \item \Character{prevlover} forgets how its artifact, \Clue{clue:truthseeing}, works.

    \item \Character{enforcer} no longer remembers the warning, the ritual, or the agreement
    
    \item \Character{buttler} forgets, for each time it accepted a bribe, who bribed it and why.
    
    \item \Character{vampirehead} did not forget anything specific.
    
    \item \Character{ghoul} did not forget anything specific.
    
    \item \Character{usurper} is the most affected. It forgot all of the details regarding its notes and other items in its study.
    
    \item \Character{traitor} forgets what exactly was the poison that was capable of killing vampires.
    
    \item \Character{supremacist} did not forget anything specific.
    
    \item \Character{newcomer} did not forget anything specific.
    
    \item \Character{lover} did not forget anything specific.
\end{itemize}

\timestamp{At 17:00}, \Character{traitor} executes the ritual. As \Character{traitor} was not really aware of what it wanted, this leads to the misplacement throughout the castle of items of \Character{usurper}'s study. This is the lore reason for most of the clues being in the places they are. This produces \Clue{clue:stealritualleftovers}.


\timestamp{At 21:00}, \Character{lover} calls \Character{newcomer}. \Character{newcomer} goes outside, and \Character{vampirehead} notices this. \Character{traitor} also notices that \Character{lover} is not in their house.

\Character{newcomer} tells \Character{lover} that their relationship will have to end, as \Character{vampirehead} will want their relationship to continue. Maybe one day in the future. \Character{newcomer} and \Character{lover} spend one last night together.

\paragraph{The day of the party}

\timestamp{At 8:00}, \Character{vampirehead} and \Character{newcomer} have a discussion. \Character{vampirehead} wants \Character{newcomer} to tell where it was the night before, but \Character{newcomer} refuses.

\timestamp{At 11:00}, \Character{lover} and \Character{newcomer} have an outside discussion, where \Character{lover} does not want the relationship to end like this. \Character{buttler} and \Character{usurper} both notice this, without noticing each other.

When this discussion ends, without anyone else seeing, \Character{buttler} offers \Character{lover} the possibility of it being quiet about this conversation, if the price is right. \Character{lover} accepts and pays.
