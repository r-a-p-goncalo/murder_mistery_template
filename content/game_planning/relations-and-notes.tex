\begin{itemize}
  \item[] \textit{Origins and the early household}

  \item \timestamp{Thousands of years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} became a vampire after wishing to be free of humanity. Its later loneliness is why it began building a family.

  \item \timestamp{Three thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} met the power-hungry human \Character{usurper}. \Character{usurper} devised the blood-based potion that turns a willing human into a family member; the vampire whose blood is used can add terms to the turning contract.

  \item Over the following millennium, \Character{usurper}'s pursuit of mystical power increasingly alienated \Character{vampirehead}. Its study became deliberately chaotic and full of encrypted notes and scattered artifacts, so that \Character{vampirehead} could not easily inspect its research.

  \item \timestamp{Two thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} brought \Character{enforcer} into the family and made it second only to the head for enforcing the newly written \Clue{rulebook}. A family vote that finds a vampire guilty of breaking a rule strips that vampire's powers.

  \item The rulebook accidentally provides that, if \Character{vampirehead} loses its powers, \Character{usurper} inherits them. \Character{usurper} notices both this succession path and \Character{vampirehead}'s ability to create magical artifacts.

  \item \timestamp{A few decades after Christ's death}, the competent but greedy merchant \Character{buttler} joins to manage the household and finances. \Character{enforcer} openly wishes for a butler of its own.

  \item \timestamp{In 100 AD}, \Character{enforcer} falls in love with the human jester \Character{ghoul}. Because the rules forbid vampires from having human lovers, \Character{enforcer} asks \Character{vampirehead} to turn \Character{ghoul}.

  \item The turning is secret, attended only by \Character{vampirehead} and \Character{enforcer}. It uses \Character{enforcer}'s blood, allowing \Character{enforcer} to add the binding term that \Character{ghoul} must always obey its orders. \Character{enforcer} then orders \Character{ghoul} to report every relevant fact it learns about any family member, forever.

  \item During that ceremony, \Character{buttler} notices that \Character{ghoul}'s flask does not contain the expected potion. \Character{vampirehead} pays \Character{buttler} to remain silent, establishing the first of several bribes that \Character{buttler} accepts.

  \item As later family members are exiled or killed for plotting against \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{usurper} learns that overthrowing the head will be difficult. While executing especially gruesome traitors in \Character{enforcer}'s absence, it discovers that the forbidden potion kills a vampire.

  \item \timestamp{In 500 AD}, the dictator \Character{supremacist} joins the family. It secretly loves \Character{vampirehead}, treats vampires well, and is made the final judge of whether humans may join the family because of its hatred of humans.

  \item \timestamp{In 700 AD}, the warrior \Character{lover} joins as the family's protector. \timestamp{In 1000 AD}, the investigator \Character{prevlover} joins.

  \item \timestamp{In 1400 AD}, \Character{traitor} joins because it and \Character{lover} are in love, despite concern that \Character{traitor}'s good-samaritan nature does not fit the family.

  \item In the centuries after joining, \Character{traitor} begins conspiring with the \outsideentity{church}: it wants vampires cured or destroyed and vulnerable humans protected. \Character{lover} still loves \Character{traitor}, while \Character{traitor} fears that \Character{lover} may have discovered the conspiracy.

  \item \Character{usurper} becomes known as the helpful family member, while secretly wanting \Character{vampirehead}'s powers. It jokes that a vampire who takes the forbidden potion a second time dies, directing the joke at \Character{traitor}; in truth it fears \Character{traitor} might kill \Character{vampirehead} before \Character{usurper} can inherit the powers.

  \item \Character{supremacist}'s ideology hardens into the belief that humans should be killed or kept as cattle, but it never confesses its love to \Character{vampirehead}. Meanwhile, \Character{ghoul} grows resentful at being treated as a joke, falls out of love with \Character{enforcer}, and wants freedom from its binding orders---which it believes \Character{enforcer}'s death would provide.

  \item[] \textit{The last two years}

  \item \timestamp{Two years before the party}, \Character{vampirehead} meets the human \Character{newcomer} and invites it to a family party. The family assumes \Character{vampirehead} is courting \Character{newcomer} and intends to turn it, although the rules prevent their relationship.

  \item \Character{supremacist} is jealous of \Character{newcomer} and opposes its joining. \Character{vampirehead} insists that \Character{newcomer} should become family and threatens to strip \Character{supremacist}'s powers.

  \item Behind \Character{vampirehead}'s back, \Character{newcomer} and \Character{prevlover} begin a romantic relationship.

  \item \timestamp{One year before the party}, \Character{newcomer} begins a relationship with \Character{lover} and leaves \Character{prevlover}, saying that what they were doing was wrong. \Character{vampirehead} remains unaware of both affairs and deliberately keeps its distance from \Character{newcomer} to avoid breaking the rules.

  \item \timestamp{A couple of weeks before the party}, \Character{usurper} offers to help \Character{vampirehead} prepare. \Character{vampirehead} asks for helpers the day before the party; only \Character{usurper}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{newcomer} agree. The others realize that \Character{usurper}'s empty study will be an opportunity to steal its artifacts.

  \item[] \textit{The day before the party}

  \item \timestamp{01:00}: \Character{ghoul}, \Character{prevlover}, \Character{traitor}, and \Character{supremacist} enter \Character{usurper}'s study while \Character{usurper} is away.

  \item \timestamp{01:00}: Seeking freedom from \Character{enforcer}, \Character{ghoul} steals the ritual ``To change the future'' from \Character{usurper}'s study. Its later use leaves \Clue{clue:futritualleftovers}.

  \item \timestamp{01:00}: Seeking a way to be with \Character{newcomer} again, \Character{prevlover} steals ``To change and know a person'' from the same study. \Character{ghoul} sees the theft. \Character{prevlover} also drops its mystery-solving artifact, \Clue{clue:truthseeing}; the ritual later leaves \Clue{clue:memritualleftovers}.

  \item \timestamp{01:00}: \Character{traitor} searches the study for \Clue{forbiddenpotion} so it can kill vampires, but cannot find it. Instead, it steals ``Make what is theirs someone else's,'' wrongly believing it will steal a target's possessions. In fact, the ritual displaces every item belonging to its target; its later use leaves \Clue{clue:stealritualleftovers}.

  \item \timestamp{01:00}: \Character{supremacist} steals \Clue{clue:truth_potion}. It believes the potion kills humans but not vampires; in fact, a vampire who drinks it can command truthful answers from sentient beings.

  \item \timestamp{08:00}: \Character{supremacist} puts half of the truth potion into wine intended only for \Character{newcomer}, leaving \Clue{clue:poisoned_wine} and retaining the remaining half. It tells \Character{buttler} that it now approves of \Character{newcomer}, claims the special wine is a gift, and pays \Character{buttler} to hide both its visit and supposed change of heart.

  \item \timestamp{12:00}: \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{newcomer}, \Character{buttler}, and \Character{usurper} have lunch. Before \Character{buttler} serves \Character{supremacist}'s wine, \Character{usurper} replaces it with its own ``quality'' wine, which contains the forbidden potion. This is \Character{newcomer}'s first turning dose.

  \item \timestamp{15:00}: \Character{ghoul} performs ``To change the future'' and sees \Character{newcomer}'s future death. Bound by \Character{enforcer}'s standing order, \Character{ghoul} must disclose what it learns about the family. The vision, together with the timing, reveals that \Character{newcomer} has already been turned by \Character{usurper}'s wine.

  \item \timestamp{16:00}: Wanting to avert a problem but unwilling to reveal \Character{ghoul}'s theft to \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{enforcer} calls \Character{prevlover}. It offers to overlook the theft if \Character{prevlover} surrenders the ritual, which \Character{enforcer} intends to use on \Character{newcomer} to learn why it will die. \Character{prevlover} fears that this would expose its relationship with \Character{lover} and cost it its powers.

  \item \timestamp{16:15}: \Character{prevlover} panics and uses the ritual on \Character{enforcer}'s memory, leaving \Clue{clue:memritualleftovers}. The ritual unexpectedly erases random memories across the family, especially memories of \Character{usurper}'s study and artifacts, including the rules in \Clue{rulebook}.

  \item After the memory ritual, \Character{prevlover} no longer knows how \Clue{clue:truthseeing} works; \Character{enforcer} forgets the warning, ritual, and agreement; \Character{buttler} forgets every briber and reason for a bribe; \Character{usurper} loses nearly all details of its notes and study items; and \Character{traitor} forgets which poison kills vampires. \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{ghoul}, \Character{supremacist}, \Character{newcomer}, and \Character{lover} lose nothing specific.

  \item \timestamp{17:00}: \Character{traitor} performs ``Make what is theirs someone else's.'' Because it has not defined what it wants, the ritual scatters \Character{usurper}'s study items throughout the castle, explaining why many clues are found away from the study and leaving \Clue{clue:stealritualleftovers}.

  \item \timestamp{21:00}: \Character{lover} calls \Character{newcomer} outside. \Character{vampirehead} notices \Character{newcomer} leave, and \Character{traitor} notices \Character{lover} is absent. \Character{newcomer} ends the relationship, believing \Character{vampirehead} will want it to continue, but the two spend one last night together.

  \item[] \textit{The day of the party and the established starting state}

  \item \timestamp{08:00}: \Character{vampirehead} asks \Character{newcomer} where it was the previous night; \Character{newcomer} refuses to say.

  \item \timestamp{11:00}: \Character{lover} asks \Character{newcomer}, outside, not to end their relationship. \Character{buttler} and \Character{usurper} each see the conversation without noticing the other witness. Afterwards, \Character{buttler} privately sells \Character{lover} its silence, and \Character{lover} pays.

  \item At the party, \Character{vampirehead} privately carries out the official turning ceremony with \Character{newcomer}, unaware that \Character{usurper}'s wine had already turned it. \Character{newcomer} later dies after receiving the turning potion a second time; \Character{vampirehead} announces that the potion was administered before the body is found.

  \item \Character{usurper}'s hidden first dose makes \Character{vampirehead} appear responsible for \Character{newcomer}'s death, while also advancing \Character{usurper}'s long-standing ambition to inherit \Character{vampirehead}'s powers.
\end{itemize}
