\paragraph{Background}

\timestamp{In 200 AD}, I joined this family as its protector. I was a warrior before I became a vampire, and I remain the person the family expects to stand between it and danger. I have also loved \Character{traitor} for centuries; that love is why it was welcomed into the family despite the doubts of others.

\timestamp{A year ago}, I began a secret relationship with \Character{newcomer}. I know \Character{vampirehead} has been courting it, and I know the rules forbid a vampire from loving a human. That makes what we have dangerous, but I will not pretend it means nothing to me.

I have seen the family turn ugly when power, rank, and old grudges are involved. If someone harmed \Character{newcomer}, I want the truth---and I will not quietly accept blame for loving it.

\paragraph{GM Notes}

\Character{lover}'s relationship with \Character{newcomer} provides a strong emotional motive and a rule-breaking scandal, but it has no part in the day-before plan. Its long relationship with \Character{traitor} can complicate loyalties when the church conspiracy is uncovered. Use the character as an innocent but protective suspect who can corroborate \Character{traitor}'s older connection to the family.
