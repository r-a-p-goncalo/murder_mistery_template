\BackgroundHeading{Background}

\timestamp{Three thousand years ago}, I was a human with an appetite for mysteries that ordinary life could never satisfy. I studied old rites, unusual substances, and every rumour of magic I could find. That is how I met \Character{vampirehead}. She was lonely, powerful, and tired of being the only creature of her kind. I was curious enough to speak to her as an equal.

Together, we developed a potion made with a her blood. A willing human who drinks it joins the donor's family and gains its gifts and weaknesses. The donor may also set terms for the new vampire. \Character{vampirehead} had no interest in using that power.

I became \Character{vampirehead}'s first companion, and for a thousand years we were close. We travelled, played cruel little games with the world, and enjoyed the freedoms no human could imagine. But I was not satisfied.

My study became the center of my work. I filled it with notes, rare ingredients, unfinished rituals, and artifacts that no sensible person would leave unattended. I keep many of the notes encrypted. \Character{vampirehead} dislikes the disorder and suspects it hides ambition. She is not wrong, but suspicion is not proof. So I learned to be pleasant, useful, and impossible to dismiss. A helpful hand is rarely examined as closely as a clenched fist.

\timestamp{Two thousand years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} introduced a new vampire. Both judge and enforcer, \Character{enforcer} was added to the family. \Character{vampirehead} gifted her with a newly created \Clue{rulebook. The book made the family's laws real: a vampire judged guilty could lose her powers. While the head treated the rules as an administrative detail, I read their consequences carefully. The hierarchy gives the closest vampire a claim to the head's powers if she is stripped of them for a crime. That vampire is me. \Character{vampirehead} created her own succession without noticing it. I did not forget.


Over the centuries, I watched new vampires arrive, scheme, fail, and sometimes die. When \Character{enforcer} was too busy to carry out a sentence, I was asked to do it. During one such execution, I discovered the secret hidden in the potion I had helped create: a vampire who receives it a second time dies almost at once. I recorded the result among my notes. Knowledge like that should never be wasted.

The family calls me its little helper, which suits me perfectly. I have supplied remedies, repaired small enchantments, and listened kindly when someone needed advice.

\timestamp{Two months ago}, \Character{vampirehead} announced that \Character{newcomer} would join the family at tonight's party. Her affection for him was obvious to anyone willing to look. I offered to help prepare the gathering, then made sure everyone knew I would be away from my study. If a few people chose that opportunity to steal from it, they would have their own secrets to explain. A house full of guilty people is a house full of useful distractions.

\timestamp{Yesterday, at 12:00}, \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{newcomer}, \Character{buttler}, and I had lunch together. When \Character{buttler} was about to serve the wine, I stopped her and offered a finer bottle of my own for \Character{newcomer}. I had paid her to make the substitution and to keep it private. \Character{newcomer} drank the potion in that wine and became a vampire without realizing it. \Character{vampirehead} believes she will turn him tonight. In truth, her correct potion will be his second dose, and it will kill him.

Later yesterday, something damaged my memory. I can no longer account for every note or artifact that was in my study, and by the evening its contents had been scattered through the castle. That is inconvenient, but it does not change what matters. I remember the plan, the wine, and the future I intend to claim.

\timestamp{This morning, at 11:00}, I saw \Character{lover} and \Character{newcomer} speaking privately outside. Their body language was far too intimate for an innocent conversation. I did not see \Character{buttler}, but she may have seen them too. That secret could be useful, especially if anyone begins asking why \Character{newcomer} might have enemies.

When \Character{newcomer} dies, grief will make everyone look for the simplest answer. \Character{vampirehead} will have given him the final potion. \Character{supremacist} has always objected to him joining the family. \Character{traitor} conspires with the church, and \Character{lover} had a reason to fear losing him. I need only be sympathetic, help the family search for the truth, and make certain nobody looks too closely at a generous bottle of wine.

\GMNotes{

\Character{usurper} is the actual killer. Its plan is to secretly turn \Character{newcomer} with the lunch wine, then let \Character{vampirehead} administer a correct second potion and appear responsible for the death. The strongest route to the solution combines \Character{buttler}'s account of the bottle swap, the double-dose effect in the forbidden-potion instructions, and \Clue{clue:hierarchy_page}, which gives \Character{usurper} a succession motive. It should deny everything, redirect suspicion to the poison, the church plot, and the head, and never volunteer its knowledge of the first dose.
