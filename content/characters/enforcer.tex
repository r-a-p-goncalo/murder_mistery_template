\CharacterSheet{enforcer}{Enforcer}{

\shortdescription{The exacting keeper of the family's law, whose authority is second only to the head's.}

\ObjectivesHeading{Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Find \Character{newcomer}'s murderer and see it voted out. (\PositiveReward{1}, \NegativeReward{1})

  \item Recover the rule book. (\PositiveReward{1}, \NegativeReward{1})
  
  \item Identify cases of family members who have broken the laws established by the rulebook.  (\PositiveReward{1}, \NegativeReward{1}) for each case

\end{itemize}

\LosingConditionsHeading{Losing conditions}
Fail to vote out \Character{newcomer}'s murderer, if there is one.

\SecretsHeading{Secrets}
\begin{itemize}

  \item \Character{ghoul} was turned with my blood and must obey any instruction I give as an order.
  
  \item I offered some kind of immunity deal in trade of something, even if I can't remember details.

\end{itemize}

\ActionsHeading{Actions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Be formal, cold, and controlling. Turn every accusation into a case: demand names, times, witnesses, and consequences.

  \item When you need something from \Character{ghoul}, state a clear order. It cannot refuse one, although revealing that power may create trouble.
\end{itemize}

\BackgroundHeading{Background}

\timestamp{Two thousand years ago}, I was a normal human with normal needs. I needed the world to not be so chaotic, I needed people to be considerate, I needed consequences for those who failed their community.

One day, \Character{vampirehead} brought me into its family because it wanted someone to make its will into law. This still is the most special night, and party, of my life. She wrote the \Clue{rulebook} then and there: a family member whom the family finds guilty of breaking a rule loses its powers. My word would carry more weight than anyone's except the head's. Justice and control suited me perfectly.

I would not really be relevant for a long time, as \Character{vampirehead} was not in a hurry to add new family members. While \Character{vampirehead}, \Character{usurper} and me were the only vampires in existence, I had fun with my immortality and powers, even if I could only comfortably have fun outside the touch of sunlight. 

\timestamp{Decades after the death of Christ}, \Character{buttler} was added to the family in a similar party as me. Previously a merchant, now a servant of \Character{vampirehead}.


\timestamp{In 100 AD}, while travelling, I met \Character{ghoul}, a charming human jester. I loved him, but the rules forbade a vampire from taking a human lover. I asked \Character{vampirehead} to turn it. I also asked that its potion be made with my blood. I told the head that it would bring us closer. The truth was that I wanted certainty, I wanted someone that could not leave my control. The contract makes \Character{ghoul} obey any instruction I give as an order.

Only \Character{ghoul} and I attended that turning. Afterwards, I gave \Character{ghoul} a standing order: he must tell me any relevant information he learns about every member of our family, for all time. He has made an excellent source of information, whether he enjoys the role or not. He's always giving the aura of being just a clumsy, funny, entertainer, so people would not really expect him to work as a spy.

Over the centuries, the family has acquired more and more members. I learned every rule, repeated them at gatherings, and hid the book safely in the castle. I was trusted to resolve doubts, to name breaches, and to keep the family from becoming a rabble of immortals. When new members proved treacherous, I handled their sentences and stripped their powers. When I was too occupied, \Character{vampirehead} found other solutions.

Since then, members were added less frequently.

\Character{supremacist} was a ruler that was added to help choose new family elements. Maybe due to the fact that, even as a human, she treated our family well. She was added in \timestamp{500 AD}.

\Character{lover} was a warrior that could help us in military operations. He joined in \timestamp{700 AD}.

\Character{prevlover} was someone that could help us in investigations. He joined in \timestamp{1000 AD}. He and I worked a lot of times together. Honestly, he was more useful due to the artifact he had than his actual intelligence. Even if I can't quite remember what the artifact was.

\timestamp{In 1400 AD}, \Character{traitor} was added. It was a controversial addition. \Character{traitor} was some kind of public good person, not really in line with what the family needed. \Character{lover} pushed it enough for \Character{vampirehead} to accept her. I didn't, don't and will never trust her.

\timestamp{Two years ago}, \Character{vampirehead} introduced a human to the family at a party, \Character{newcomer}. Nothing worth caring.

\timestamp{Sometime a year ago}, someone stole a page of the rulebook. I was furious, and thought that someone seemed liked the perfect candidate. For some reason, I can't really remember who it was. Still, I did not want to rush anything. I asked \Character{prevlover} for help, and we both went and tracked some incriminating messages of house of the suspect. I don't remember the messages. \Character{prevlover} was the one with our notes.


\timestamp{Two months ago}, In the usual fashion,\Character{vampirehead} marked \timestamp{16/7/2026} as the date of the party to add \Character{newcomer} to the family, and all vampires, as was usual in the family, had to come.

\timestamp{A couple of weeks ago}, \Character{vampirehead} questioned if anyone else besides her, \Character{buttler} and \Character{newcomer} would want to help. She said that \Character{usurper} offered to help, and would reach the castle two nights before the party.


\timestamp{Yesterday, sometime after 16:00}, I felt something weird. The rules that I preached and executed for so long were lost to my memory. I'm not capable of recollecting most of that day. I vaguely remember offering some kind of immunity deal to an element of the family.

\GMNotes{

\paragraph{Why would someone suspect this character?}

\Character{enforcer} has unusual authority, has hidden the \Clue{rulebook}, and secretly controls \Character{ghoul} through a magical command. Its missing afternoon gives it an apparent opportunity to conceal a motive or an attack, while its willingness to handle punishment makes a cold-blooded theory plausible.

\paragraph{How can innocence of this character be proved?}

The lost-memory incident occurred at \timestamp{16:15}, hours after \Character{newcomer} secretly received \Character{usurper}'s first dose of potion at lunch. \Character{enforcer} neither arranged the wine nor took part in the lunch. Evidence from \Character{prevlover} and the memory-ritual leftovers shows that its forgotten actions were an attempt to prevent a predicted death, not cause one.

\paragraph{What is this character's role in the story and game?}

\Character{enforcer} gives the investigation a procedural voice. It can turn rumours into accusations, make the \Clue{rulebook}'s consequences public once recovered, and use a direct order to force \Character{ghoul} to disclose relevant information. Its damaged memory also gives \Character{prevlover} a serious but non-murderous crime to hide.

\paragraph{How can this character point into the intended solution?}

If the players uncover the memory ritual or pressure \Character{prevlover}, establish that \Character{enforcer} had been acting on \Character{ghoul}'s warning about \Character{newcomer}'s death. A clear order can make \Character{ghoul} repeat what it reported. That recovered timeline directs attention away from the later memory ritual and toward what happened at lunch, especially the wine \Character{usurper} supplied.
